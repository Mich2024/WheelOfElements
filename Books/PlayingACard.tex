\subsection{The Action Deck}
%Dezenter reminder über das level up mit Seitenzahl zu level up segment
The action deck contains all action cards you can draw and subsequently play during combat.\\
As a deckbuilding game, the action deck will continue to evolve during gameplay. \textit{(See Character Building, page \pageref{Section_CharacterBuilding})}\\
Your first action deck is composed of all the action cards from your ancestry, and your two classes. 

\textbf{Deckbuilding} Your action deck consists of exactly \textbf{16 cards}. When you put new cards in, you must remove an equal amount. You keep all the cards you acquire at your disposal, and you may return them to your deck later - but some other cards will have to be removed if you choose to add the old ones back into your deck.

\subsection{Playing a Card}
\label{Section_PlayingACard}
\begin{figure}[ht]
	\begin{minipage}[t][6.5cm][t]{0.5\textwidth}
		\textbf{Play} - You may play up to \textbf{two cards} per turn. \\
		
		\textbf{Pay} - Cards either have \textbf{no} cost, an \textbf{optional} element cost or a \textbf{mandatory} element cost. To pay an element cost, move the stated amount of element markers from the corresponding field on the wheel back into the stash. If the cost is mandatory, you may not play it without paying the element cost.
		
		\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
			When playing a card, you execute all effects from top to bottom, going line by line. All effects \textbf{must} be used if possible. While you are executing a card, you must finish before another player can interact with the targets you are dealing with.
		\end{tcolorbox}

\textbf{Attack \symAttack} - To perform an attack, first \textbf{target} an enemy to attack (\textit{see Targeting, page \pageref{Section_Targeting}}). Then, \textbf{roll} your ancestry dice (\textit{see Ancestry dice, page \pageref{Section_AncestryDice}}) and add the result to the printed attack value. Lastly, \textbf{apply} the resulting damage to the enemy. Track this damage via the damage cubes. The bronze cubes represent 1 damage, the silver ones 5, the gold ones 10 and the green ones 50.

\textbf{Elements \symFire \symEarth \symMetal \symWater \symWood} - To generate elements, check how many element symbols are pictured as an action, and move that many markers from the box to the wheel, on the corresponding field. All players share the elements in wheel.


\textbf{Boons \& Banes} - To apply a boon or bane, choose a target, then put the listed number of boons or banes on the target. \textit{see Boons \& Banes, page \pageref{Section_BoonsBanes}}

\textbf{Text} - Quite a few cards have text with longer instructions. Simply follow the instruction when playing the card. Those cards are usually very unique in what they do.


\textbf{Opt Paid} - Cards with optional costs have a line with the word OPT going through the card with extra actions below the line.
If the optional cost was paid, the actions below the line must be used. If the cost was not paid, they cannot be used.

\textbf{Blaze \symBlaze} - Cards with blaze also have a line, but with the text Blaze:X instead of OPT.
If you play one of these cards as the  \textbf{first card} on your turn, you must \textbf{randomly} discard X cards, and use the actions below the line.
When played as the second card in a turn, the blaze action does nothing -- no cards are discarded, and the actions below the line are ignored.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
	After playing a card, put it into your discard pile.
	
\end{tcolorbox}




\subsection{The Ancestry Dice}
These dice modify the results of your \symAttack attacks.
Every time you pick a class, you will get an additional ancestry die. You always use all dice at once.
The ancestry dice can have four effects. Some sides are blank and have no effect. Each die also has one side with an icon of the ancestry it belongs to

\label{Section_AncestryDice}

The Ancestry Dice act as a source of random chance. Fighting is a risky business, and sometimes an attack may take an unexpected turn.
You usually start with two Ancestry dice.
Over the course of the game, you add more Ancestry Dice by leveling up and covering your ancestry tableau with class tiles.


\textbf{Attacks \symAttack} - Whenever you \symAttack attack, roll your Ancestry Dice.
First, if any \textbf{Boons or Banes} have been rolled, apply them to yourself or the target. Immediately use Boons \& Banes such as \symPowerful Powerful and \symVulnerable Vulnerable when calculating the damage.
Second, \textbf{add all numbers} together and add them to the printed value of the attack.
Afterwards, apply this damage to the targeted enemy. You cannot deal less than 0 damage.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
	%If you have played table top RPGs before, you may know the concept from rolling a 20 sided dice to determine the outcome of an action.
	Only attacks use the ancestry dice by default, nothing else.
\end{tcolorbox}


\textbf{Advantage \symAdvantage} - Advantage is a boon that gives you an additional chance to hit good modifiers.
After having rolled your dice but before applying any effect from them, including Boons \& Banes, you may choose a number of dice  \textbf{equal or less than the amount of advantage} you have and roll them again. Advantage is not consumed in this process, and can be used for any ancestry dice roll until it is removed at the end of your turn.

\textbf{AOE} - \textbf{A}rea \textbf{O}f \textbf{E}ffect cards may target  \textbf{enemies} that are \textbf{adjacent} to one another (\textit{See below}), in the pattern shown on the emblem. Enemies facing  \textbf{different players} are  \textbf{never} considered as adjacent to each other, and AOEs may not flow over to another player.
\textbf{Repeat} - When you target multiple enemies like this, all attacks and banes of the card are repeated for each enemy. You will re-roll all dice and reapply banes for each enemy separately.
