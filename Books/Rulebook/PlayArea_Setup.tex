The play area is composed of the following components.
\begin{enumerate}	
	\item \textbf{Action draw pile} - Shuffle your action deck before combat begins to create your draw pile
	\item \textbf{Action discard pile} - After you play a card, it is placed face up in this pile
	\item \textbf{Attack Dice} - All your current attack dice are being placed here
	\item \textbf{Ancestry Tableau} - The tableau helps you track your decisions as you level up, your health pool, and contains an overview for the combat turn
	\item \textbf{Enemy Zone} - Enemies go here, with the configuration described in the storybook
	\item \textbf{The Wheel of Elements} - Many cards use a shared resource called elements. This resource is tracked here. Only the inner part is used.
	\item \textbf{The Storybook} - This book contains the story and encounter setup 
	\item \textbf{The Event Deck} - Between encounters, events will be drawn from this deck to mix up the way the following encounters unfold
	\item \textbf{Markers} - Cubes for tracking the enemy life, and markers to track boons \& banes
\end{enumerate}

	To construct their first action and modifier deck, each \textbf{player} follows these steps:
	\begin{enumerate}	
		\item \textbf{Ancestry} - Pick an ancestry
		\item \textbf{Class 1} - Pick your first rank 1 class
		\item \textbf{Class 2} - Pick a second rank 1 class
		\item \textbf{Action Deck} - Shuffle all action cards from your ancestry and two classes together. This forms your action deck
		\item \textbf{Ancestry Tableau} - Take your chosen ancestry tableau
		\item \textbf{Attack Dice} - Take the blue and red attack dice from your ancestry, as indicated by the tableau
		\item \textbf{Class Tiles} - Place the two class tiles you chose into the top left rectangular slot on your ancestry tableau
		\item \textbf{Starting Life} - Calculate your starting life by adding all totals from your ancestry tableau and the two class tiles. Place a life marker on the corresponding position.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	
	\vspace{0.3cm}
	Apart from the per player setups you just did, you will also need to construct this \textbf{shared} setup in the middle:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \textbf{Wheel} - Put the Wheel of Elements in the middle
		\item \textbf{Storybook} - Select a new story from the storybook (we recommend story no.1 if it is everyone's first time playing)
		\item \textbf{Story} - Read out the first passage of the story
		\item \textbf{Encounter} - Set up the enemies corresponding to the first encounter description
		\item \textbf{Enemy Tracker} - Roll the enemy dice and place it atop the wheel. Place red cubes on ABC enemies
	\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}

\section{Ancestry and Classes}
\label{Section_AncestryAndClasses}

\textbf{Ancestry} - 
In \textit{Wheel of Elements} there are 11 different ancestries to choose from. The ancestries are dedicated to \textbf{one Boon or Bane} and often also to an element. Try experimenting to see which playstyles you like. Each ancestry comes with 7 attack dice, one passive, 6 action cards and a tableau.

\textbf{Ancestry Tableau} - your core tool for tracking character progress.
It contains the ancestry name, places for all of the class tiles, your first passive, a place to track your life, and an overview of the combat turn.


\textbf{Attack Dice} - Whenever you get a class tile, it will cover a square on your ancestry tableau.
When you cover one of the seven slots with a tile, add the printed attack dice to your dice pool.


\textbf{Placement} - The class tiles are placed in the ancestry tableau in the \textbf{leftmost open slot of their rank}.
Rank 1 classes go in the top row,
rank 2 classes in the middle row, and
rank 3 classes in the bottom row.
To begin the game, you will choose two rank 1 classes.
The respective Class tiles are always placed at the top left in the rectangle.

\textbf{Passives} - The passives you acquire will shape your gameplay immensely. Passives come in three varieties: 

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Trigger} - These passives apply at specific times, like the start of combat, or the end of your turn. You are granted a concrete benefit at the specified time
	\item \textbf{Permanent} - Some passives modify the way you perform actions permanently. For example, you might gain the ability to use your ancestry dice when applying certain Boons or Banes
	\item \textbf{Shaping} - Some passives modify what you would normally get from an ancestry or class. They might provide additional ancestry dice, more powerful cards, or simply more maximum life
\end{itemize}
