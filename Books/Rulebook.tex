\textbf{Game Overview}\\
Wheel of Elements is a cooperative multi-classing Deckbuilder. Become an indomitable tank, a mage of immense elemental power or something between, like a berserking druid. Beat three battles of increasing difficulty and prove your mettle.\\

Wheel of Elements is a \textbf{Deckbuilder}. Over the course of the game you will add to and remove cards from your deck. Many cards synergize, letting you get creative with your combinations. \\

Your cards also interact with the \textbf{Modifier Deck}, which adds damage or a special effect to each attack you perform.\\

It is also a \textbf{Multi-Classing} game. Each species and class comes with cards and a passive effect that will twist your gameplay. You can rank up old classes to improve your strengths and adopt new ones to cover your weaknesses.\\

\textbf{Playtime}\\
The game consists of three fights with random events and deckbuilding sessions between them. A full game takes about 2.5 hours. Plan in some extra time for your first game.\\

\textbf{Winning \& Losing}\\
\textbf{Victory} - Victory is achieved when all enemies from the story are defeated.\\
\textbf{Loss} - You lose when every player is defeated during one fight.\\

\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
\textbf{Rule 0:} The game is about having fun. If you and your playgroup decide that some change to the rules would increase your fun, experiment with it!
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Setup}

\begin{minipage}[t][10cm][t]{0.5\textwidth}
To construct their first action and modifer deck, each \textbf{player} follows these steps:
\begin{enumerate}	
	\item \textbf{Species} - Pick a Species
	\item \textbf{Class 1} - Pick a your first rank 1 class
	\item \textbf{Class 2} - Pick a second rank 1 class
	\item \textbf{Modifier Deck} - Shuffle your species starter modifier cards and your base deck together. This forms your Modifier Deck
	\item \textbf{Character Tableau} - Take a Character Tableau and place the two Class Shingles you chose into your Character Tableau
	\item \textbf{Action Deck} - Shuffle all action cards from your species and two classes together. This forms your Action Deck
\end{enumerate}


\vspace{0.3cm}
The \textbf{shared} play area is constructed as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Wheel} - Put the Wheel of Elements in the middle
	\item \textbf{Storybook} - Select a new story from the Storybook (We recommend Story Nr.1 if it is everyone's first time playing)
	\item \textbf{Story} - Read out the first passage of the story
	\item \textbf{Encounter} - Set up the enemies corresponding to the first encounter descrpition
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}	
\subsection*{Setup for encounters 2 and 3}
After you beat each encounter, you level up, as described in Section \pageref{Section_CharacterBuilding}.
Do some deckbuilding, and you proceed to the next encounter.
After the first encounter, read out passage 2 from the storybook and set up the second encounter.
When beating encounter 2, repeat with passage 3 and encounter 3, and when you beat encounter 3,  read the last passage from the story. When you reach this point, you win.

\section{Character Building} \label{Section_CharacterBuilding}

\textbf{The Character Tableau} - your core tool for tracking character progress.
Every Tableau is dedicated to a different species with unique modifiers and passives.
It contains the species name, places for all of the class shingles, your first passive, a place to put your life counter and a reminder text for the turn order.

\textbf{Modifiers} - Whenever you get a class shingle, it will cover a square on your character sheet.
Each slot for a shingle has changes to the modifier deck printed on it.
When you cover a slot with a shingle, add or remove the printed modifiers to your modifier deck.

\textbf{Progression} - To begin the game, you will choose two rank 1 classes.
The respective Class shingles are always placed in the top left in the rectangle.
Between every two encounters and after the event, you level up.
When you level up, you choose either a new class at rank 1, or a class you already have take one rank higher than the one you currently have. Add the new Classes Shingle to your tableau, take the cards of that class, and increase your maximum life by the amount stated on the new class.
You also gain a new passive. These effects are varied ways to shape your gameplay.
Most of the effects trigger at specified points during your turn. Some offer static benefits, like the one shown for the half elf which massively improves your modifier deck.

\textbf{Placement} - The class shingles have to be placed in the leftmost slot of their rank.
Rank 1 classes go in the top row.
Rank 2 classes go in the middle row.
Rank 3 classes go in the bottom row.

\section{Combat}
Most of your time will be spent in combat. During combat, your choices during levelling up and deckbuilding are tested against chalenging foes.
\subsection*{Start} 
When entering an encounter from the Storybook, perform this setup once:\\
\begin{minipage}[t][2.7cm][t]{\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Shuffle} - Shuffle your Action deck and your modifier deck 
\item \textbf{Elemental Wheel} - Empty it
\item \textbf{Life} - Set your life to maximum.
\item \textbf{Enemies} - Setup enemies based on the Storybook.
\item \textbf{Roll} - Roll the Monster Die
\item \textbf{Passives} - Start of combat passives trigger
	
\end{itemize}
\end{minipage}
The first turn goes to the players, followed by an enemy turn. Alternate between player and enemy turn until either every enemy or player is defeated.
\subsection*{End}
When all enemies are defeated, you win the encounter. If all encounters are won, you complete the story successfully.
If all players are defeated you loose the story. In case some players are defeated, but the encounter is won, those players incur no penalty, return to full life and win together with the party.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
	When an enemy is defeated, immediately remove that card from the playing area.
\end{tcolorbox}


\subsection*{Player Turn}

All players play simultaneously. You can coordinate with other players to sequence cards in your favour. The actions they do follow a pattern, but synchronization is only necessary when moving to and from the enemy turn.

\begin{itemize}

	\item \textbf{Draw} - Draw up to your maximum hand size. If your draw pile is empty, shuffle it\\
	
	\item \textbf{Passives} - Start of turn passives trigger. Players may decide order
	\item \textbf{Play} - Players may play up to 2 cards each turn
	To play a card:\\
	\hspace*{1cm} \textbf{Pay} any element cost you must or want\\
	\hspace*{1cm} \textbf{Perform} the cards actions \\
	\hspace*{1cm} \textbf{Discard} the played card into the discard pile
\end{itemize}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
	After every player has finished playing their cards move to the enemies turn. Indicate this by turning your leftmost enemy by 90 degrees. Coordination is important here, speak with the group to ensure that no one is still playing out their turn.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection*{Enemy actions}
Enemies act sequentially from left to right. 
To perform the enemy turn, look at the monster die,  and have the monster perform the corresponding action.
Repeat once for every enemy.

\subsection*{Cleanup}

\begin{figure}[ht]
	\begin{minipage}[t][13cm][t]{0.5\textwidth}
		At the end of the turn, the players set up the board for the next turn.
		\begin{itemize}
			\item \textbf{Boons \& Banes} - Apply poison damage and remove any single turn boon or bane. \textit{See page \pageref{Section_BoonsBanes}} \\
			\item \textbf{Rotate} - Move your leftmost (sideways) enemy to the player to your left. This happens simultaneously. You cannot give away an enemy given to you this way. Turn the enemy given to you by your right neighbour back up straight.\\
			\item \textbf{Dead} - If you are dead, give all your remaining enemies to your left. Without an opponent to fight, all of your remaining enemies rotate to the left.
			Enemies will never move to a defeated player, and instead immediately rotate to the next living player.
			\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
				This can result into floods of enemies tearing through the rest of the party, so be careful!
			\end{tcolorbox}
			\item \textbf{Wheel} - Rotate the inner part of Wheel of Elements. This means that element markers in the fire slot become element markers in the earth slot and so forth.\\
			\item \textbf{Modifier Deck} - Shuffle the modifier discard pile back into the modifier deck, if the *0 or *2 modifiers are in the discard pile.\\
			\item \textbf{Roll} - Roll the Monster Die.

\textbf{Attack \symAttack} - To perform an attack, first select an enemy to attack.
Then, draw a modifier from you modifier deck, and add its value to the attack.
Every Deck also has a *2 and a *0. In that case, multiply your attack value by two (or  zero) after everything else was applied.
Modifiers are drawn and applied separately for each enemy attacked. This applies to multiple lines of attacks, as well as AOE attacks.

\textbf{Elements \symFire \symEarth \symMetal \symWater \symWood} - To generate elements, check how many element symbols are pictured on the card, and move that many markers from the stash to the wheel, on the corresponding field.

\textbf{Opt cost} - Cards with optional costs have a line with the word OPT going through the card with additional actions below the line.
If the optional cost was paid, the actions below the line must be used. If the cost was not paid, they cannot be used.

\textbf{Blaze \symBlaze} - Cards with blaze also have a line, but with the text Blaze:X instead of OPT.
If you play one of these cards as the first card on your turn, you must discard X cards, and use the actions below the line.
When played as the second card in a turn, the blaze action does nothing -- no cards are discarded, and the actions below the line are ignored.

\textbf{Boons \& Banes} - To apply a boon or bane, choose a target, then put the listed number of boons or banes on the target. \textit{See also page \pageref{Section_BoonsBanes}}

\textbf{Healing \symHeal} - Healing restores life by the stated amount. 

\textbf{Discard} - After playing a card, put it into your discard pile.

\subsection*{Modifiers}

The Modifier Deck acts as an a source of random chance. Fighting is a risky business, and sometimes an attack may take an unexpected turn.
The modifier deck usually has 18 cards in it, favours you slightly and improves over time. 
Over the course of the game, you can add many modifiers that are more interesting than just numbers.
\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
If you have played table top RPGs before, you may know the concept from rolling a 20 sided dice to determine the outcome of an action.
\end{tcolorbox}

\textbf{Modify all  attacks \symAttack} - All  attacks (and only attacks) are modified by default. Modification usually changes how much damage your attack does. 
To determine the final damage, draw one card from your modifier deck and add its value to the attack.

\textbf{Draw \symDraw} - If you draw a modifier with a draw symbol, you must draw another. Modifiers with the draw symbol can chain into each other. 

\textbf{Multipliers} - When you draw the *2 or the *0 multiply your attack by that value. Multiplication aways happens at the end, even if you manage to draw one such modifier before.

\textbf{Shuffle} - The two base multipliers also have a shuffle symbol on them, as a reminder for you to shuffle the Modifier Discard into your deck at the end of the turn.

\textbf{Advantage \symAdvantage} - Advantage gives you an additional chance to hit good modifiers.
When modifying an attack with advantage, reveal one modifier and if necessary perform all of the extra draws you may have.
Repeat this process for each advantage you have, and separate the drawn modifiers for each repeat into piles.
Afterwards choose the pile you like most, and apply the modifiers in that pile.

\textbf{Passive Interaction} - Some class passives allow you to modify healing or poison.
If you have one of those classes, modify your poison and heal actions  as you would modify an attack.

\textbf{Empty} - If you ever run out of modifiers in a single turn, the deck stays empty, and all further modifiers are treated as +0. Shuffle only during Cleanup.

\section{Boons \& Banes}\label{Section_BoonsBanes}
\textbf{Tracking} - Boons \& Banes are tracked with status tokens. When you apply a boon or bane to a player or an enemy, place a status token on it.

\begin{longtable}{|p{2.5cm}|p{13.7cm}|}

	\hline
	Powerful \symPowerful & Your attacks deal one extra damage. Remove powerful from players at the end of the turn. For enemies powerful persists between turns. \\
	\hline
	Advantage \symAdvantage & After revealing a modifier, reveal another. You may choose which to use. Remove advantage from players and enemies at the end of the turn.\\
	\hline
	Shield \symShield & When taking damage from an \symAttack attack (not self inflicted or poison), take one less. Remove shield from players at the end of the turn. For enemies shield persists between turns. \\
	\hline
	Flurry \symFlurry & At any point during your turn, you may spend one flurry token to perform \symAttack:1. Flurry persists between turns on both players and enemies. \\
	\hline
	Poison \symPoison & At the end of the turn, lose one life. Poison persists between turns on both players and enemies.\\
	\hline
	Vulnerable \symVulnerable & When taking damage from an attack, take one additional damage. When hitting the *2 or *0 on a vulnerable enemy, the extra damage is multiplied too. Vulnerable persists between turns on both players and enemies. \\
	\hline
	Stun \symStun & When playing a card while stunned, instead ignore all of the cards effects and remove one stun token. When an enemy is stunned they instead skip their full action to remove one stun token. Stun persists between turns. \\
	\hline



\end{longtable}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colframe=black, colback=white]
	All status tokens can stack, which results in the effect being applied multiple times.
	For example, if powerful is applied three times on you, place three powerful tokens on your character sheet and your attacks now deal three extra damage.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Optional Rules}

1.: If you find it very hectic for all players to take the turns simultaneously, you can choose to play one player at a time. In this case it may be nice for all players to look at the current players hand and discuss.

2.: With many players, looking at the monster die may be an issue for some at the table. Consider rolling a die per person, and everyone uses their own result for the monster action. Remember to synchronize the beginning of the player turn, as it is easy to fall into your own rythm.

3.: If you need a very hectic experience, don't synchronize your turns. Just play your cards as fast as you can, act for and rotate the enemies when you finish. This is not very balanced. The player that killed the most enemies wins. Try snatching elements whenever possible, it improves your K/D.
