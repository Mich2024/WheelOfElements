\textbf{Game Overview}\\
Wheel of Elements is a cooperative multi-classing deckbuilder. Become an indomitable tank, a Mage of Immense arcane Power or something between, like a berserking Druid. Beat three battles of increasing difficulty and prove your mettle.

Wheel of Elements is a \textbf{deckbuilder}. Each Race and class gives you cards which combine to a 16 card Deck. Many cards twist the rules and let you get creative with your gameplay. You may find yourself optimizing for maximum damage output, trying to keep your friends safe, or stalling the enemy forces indefinitely. Your cards also interact witht he Modifier Deck, which adds damage or a special effect to each attack you perform.

Wheel of Elements is also a \textbf{multi-classing} game. Each player starts with a species and two classes. Your species lets you make a major decision at the start of your game and governs how your modifier deck grows, whereas you can choose classes to imrove your strengths and cover your weaknesses. You Improve a class or branch our into a new one after each won combat.\\

\textbf{Playtime}\\
The Game consists of three fights with random events and deckbuilding sessions between them. A full game takes about 2 hours. Plan for some extra time your first game.\\

\textbf{Winning \& Losing}\\
Victory - Victory is achieved when all enemies from the story are defeated.\\
Loss - You lose when every player is defeated in the same encounter.\\

\section{Setup}


To construct their first action and modifer deck, each player follows these steps:
\begin{enumerate}	
	\item Pick a species
	\item Pick a rank 1 Class
	\item Pick a different rank 1 Class
	\item Shuffle all modifier cards from your base. This forms your Modifier Deck
	\item Take your Species Tableau and place the two Class Shingles you chose into your Species Tableau
	\item Shuffle all action cards from your species and two classes together. This forms your Action Deck
\end{enumerate}
The rest of the play area is constructed as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Put the Mana Wheel in the middle
	\item Select a story from the Storybook
	\item Read out the first passage of the story
	\item Set up the enemies corresponding to the first encounter descrpition
\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{Setup for encounters 2 and 3}
After you beat each encounter, you will level up, as described in Section \ref{Section_CharacterBuilding}.
This will change your decks, and you wil proceed to the next encounter.
After the first encounter, read out passage 2 from the storybook and set up the second encounter.
When beating encounter 2, repeat with passage 3 and encounter 3, and when you beat encounter 3,  read the last passage from the story. When you reach this point, you win.

\section{The Storybook}
The storybook contains all of the Stories you can play in Wheel of Elements.
Each story will take about 2 hours to play through, contain 3 Encounters and one text containing a short story for why you are fighting.

\subsection*{Stories}
Each story begins with a plot hook, which should be narrated by a player.
	The text is separated into four sections. After reading a section, start one of the three fights. After finishing a fight, read another section, draw an event, and level up. After finishing the second fight, repeat this process.

\section{Character Building} \label{Section_CharacterBuilding}

The Character Tableau is your core tool for tracking character progress.
Every Tableau is dedicated to a different species with unique modifiers passives.
It contains the species name, places for all of the class shingles, your first passive, a place to put your life counter and a reminder text for the turn order.

Whenever you egt a class shigle, it will cover a square on your character sheet.
Each slot for a shingle has changes to the modifier deck printed on it.
When you cover a slot with a shingle, add or remove the printed modifiers to your modifier deck.

To begin the game, you will choose two rank 1 classes.
The respective Class shingles are always placed in the top left in the rectangle.
Between every two encouters in the storybook, you level up.
When you level up, you choose a new class and add its shingle to your tableau, you get the cards of that class to add to your card pool, and you increase your life tracker to reflect the new maximum life.
You also gain a new passive. These effects are varied ways to shape your gameplay.
Most of the trigger at specified points during your turn. Some offer static bbenenfits, like the one shown for the half elf which massively improves your modifier deck.

The class shingles have to be placed in the leftmost slot of their rank.
Rank 1 classes go in the top row.
Rank 2 classes go in the middle row.
Rank 3 classes go in the bottom.

\subsection*{Player Turn}

\textbf{Start of Turn}\\
\textbf{Draw} - Draw up to your maximum hand size.\\
\hspace*{1cm}\textbf{Empty draw pile} - When your draw pile is empty and you need to draw, your discard pile becomes your \hspace*{1cm}new draw pile. Shuffle it.\\
\textbf{Roll} - Roll the Monster Die\\
\textbf{Start} - Start of turn abilities trigger. Players may decide order.\\
\textbf{Play} - Players may play up to 2 cards each turn.
To play a card:\\
\hspace*{1cm}Pay its mandatory element cost\\
\hspace*{1cm}Choose if you want to pay the optional costs.\\
\hspace*{1cm}Resolve the card and apply modifiers. Put the played card into the discard pile.\\
After every player has finished playing their cards, move to the enemy turn.

To perform an attack, first select an enemy to attack.
Then, draw a modifier from you modifer deck, and add its value to the attack.
Every Deck also has a *2 and a *0. In that case, multiply your attak value by two (or  zero) after everything else was applied.
Modifiers are drawn and applied separately for each enemy attacked. This applies to multiple lines of attacks, as well as AOE attacks.

To generate elemental mana, check how many mana symbols are shown on the card, and move that many markers from the stash to the wheel, on the corresponding field.

Cards with optional costs have a line with the word OPT going through the card with additional actions below the line.
If the optional cost was paid, the actions below the line must be used. If not, they cannot be used.

Cards with blaze also have a line, but with the text Blaze:X instead of OPT.
If you play one of these cards as the first in your turn, you must discard X cards, and use the actions below the line.
When palyed as the second card in a turn, the blaze actions does nothing. No cards are discarded, and the actions below the line are ignored.

To apply a boon or bane, choose a target, then put the listed number of boons or banes on the target.
All possible effects are listed below in chapter \ref{Section_BoonsBanes}.
Healing restores life by the stated amount. 

After playing a card, put it into your discard pile.

\subsection*{Modifiers}

The Modifier Deck acts as an element of randomchance. Fighting is reisky business, and sometimes an attack may take an unexpected turn.
If you ahve played table top RPGs before, you may know the concept from rolling a 20 sided dice to determine the outcome of an action.
Simmilarly, the modifier deck usually has 20 cards in it, with a slightly positive weighting at the start which improves over time. 
Over the course of the game, you can add many modifiers that are more interesting then just numbers.
The possible effects are:

\rowcolors{2}{white}{blue!10}
\begin{longtable}{|p{2cm}|p{14.2cm}|}
	
	\hline
	Draw \symDraw &  Draw another modifier and apply both. This effect can chain into multiple draws  \\
	\hline
	Element \symFire & Create the shown elements after the full card is performed \\
	\hline
	Keywords & Some races have their keywords on some of the modifiers. If so, apply the keyword after the full card is performed. \\
	\hline
	
\end{longtable}

All attacks are modified by default.
When modifying an attack, by default you draw one card from your modifier deck and add its value to the attack.
When you draw the *2 or the *0 multiplay your attack by the value. These two modifiers also have a shuffle symbol on them, as a reminder for you to shuffle the Modifier Discard into your deck at the end of the turn.
If you draw a modifer with a draw symbol, you must draw another. Modifiers with the draw symbol can chain into each other. 
If you end by drawing a *2 or *0, first apply all addition and subtraction, then multiply.

Advantage gives you an additional chance to hit good modifiers.
When modifying an attack with advantage, reveal one modifier and if necessary perform all of the extra draws you may have.
Repeat this process for each advantage you have, and separate the drawn modifiers for each repeat into piles.
Afterwards choose the pile you like most, and apply the modifers in that pile.

Some classes passives allow you to modify healing or poison.
If you have one of those classes, modify your poison and heal actions  as you would an attack.

\section{Boons \& Banes}\label{Section_BoonsBanes}
As well as other keywords.
Boons and banes are traked va status token. When you aplly a boon or bane to a player or an enemy, place a status token on it.
All status tokens can stack, which results in the effect being applied multiple times.
For example, if powerful is applied three times on you, place three powerful tokens on your character sheet and apply the effect three times.


\begin{longtable}{|p{2.5cm}|p{13.7cm}|}

	\hline
	Powerful \symPowerful & Your attacks deal one extra damage. Remove powerful from players at the end of the turn. For enemies powerful persists between turns. \\
	\hline

	\hline
	Shield \symShield & When taking damage from an attack, take one less. Remove shield from players at the end of the turn. For enemies shield persists between turns. \\
	\hline

	\hline
	Poison \symPoison & At the end of the turn, loose one life. Poison persists between turns. \\
	\hline
	Vulnerable \symVulnerable & When taking damage from an attack, take one more. Vulnerable persists between turns. \\
	\hline
	Stun \symStun & When playing a card while stunned, instead ignore all of the cards effects and remove one stun token. When an enemy is stunned they instead skip their action to remove one stun token. Stun persists between turns. \\
	\hline



\end{longtable}

If you find it very hectic for all players to take the turns simultaneously, you can choose to play one player at a time. In this case it may be nice for all players to look at the current players hand and discuss.

With many players looking at the monster dice may be an issue for some at the table. Consider rolling a dice per person, and everyone uses their own result for the monster action. Remeber to synchronize the starts of your turn though.
